# Product Vision

## Problem

Replacement research is a multi-stage evidence and decision problem. A researcher often has to:

- identify an old product from a partial model number, photo, or description;
- locate specifications for an obsolete or discontinued model;
- separate value-relevant characteristics from incidental ones;
- translate legacy technology into present-day requirements;
- find products that are actually available now;
- compare plausible candidates without rewarding size, novelty, or price for their own sake;
- determine equivalency and document a defensible rationale.

Simple shopping search is optimized for attractive products, not the nearest defensible replacement. Products that share a brand, category, appearance, size, or headline feature can still differ materially in function, positioning, and value.

## Product promise

A user should be able to enter `Samsung UN55NU6900` and receive:

- a verified original-product profile;
- explicit minimum replacement requirements;
- researched current candidates;
- the best LKQ recommendation;
- a deterministic score from 0–100 and a classification;
- a side-by-side attribute comparison;
- meaningful matches, deficits, and upgrades;
- evidence for identity and specifications;
- a current price and source when verified and recently observed.

The product does not promise that every search has a perfect match. It promises a transparent answer: the closest defensible candidate, the controlled fallback used if necessary, and what remains unknown.

## Business value

The application compresses a slow manual workflow while making the conclusion easier to review. It can reduce inconsistent product selection, surface under-matches and expensive over-matches, preserve research evidence, and allow another reviewer to reproduce the outcome.

## Initial users

- contents professionals
- insurance-related researchers
- warranty teams
- restoration companies
- procurement teams
- retailers
- consumers

Insurance work is an important use case, not the product's only identity. The central job—finding and defending a suitable current replacement—also exists in warranty, procurement, restoration, retail, and household purchasing.

## Experience principles

- Lead with the answer, but keep the derivation one click away.
- Make uncertainty and missing information visible.
- Explain requirements before presenting a winner.
- Show rejected and above-LKQ candidates when they teach the user something.
- Keep research confidence distinct from replacement score.
- Prefer a smaller set of verified candidates over a large, weakly sourced list.
- Optimize for auditability, not conversational theater.

## Long-term extensibility

The universal product/evidence contract and category extensions should eventually support many household and commercial categories. Category-specific eligibility and proximity rules must remain modular; a television's display technology and a refrigerator's installation envelope are not interchangeable generic features.

Do not dilute the contest MVP. First make TVs excellent, then demonstrate that refrigerators and AV receivers fit the same architecture with different rules.
