# AV Receiver Category Rules

The legacy [ItemAssist AV receiver research page](../reference/itemassist-av-receiver-research.html) is the main repository source for this category. Its core lesson is that two products can share a 7.2 label, brand, chassis, Atmos, and HDMI while differing substantially in real system capability and product tier.

## Relevant attributes

- channel configuration
- amplified channels
- processed channels
- rated power with measurement context
- HDMI input and output counts
- HDMI generation, bandwidth, and implemented capabilities
- 4K/8K modes and passthrough support
- HDR formats
- ARC/eARC
- Dolby Atmos
- DTS:X
- room-correction platform/tier
- Ethernet/networking, Bluetooth, and Wi-Fi
- multi-zone support
- multichannel and subwoofer pre-outs where relevant
- phono input where relevant
- generation-relative product positioning

## Normalize architecture before scoring

Distinguish amplified channels from processed channels. A device that processes 7.2 but internally amplifies five channels is not interchangeable out of the box with one that amplifies seven. Treat processor-plus-separate-amplification as a different architecture, not simply a higher integrated-AVR tier.

Separate headline HDMI version language from actual bandwidth, video modes, HDR support, eARC, and input/output implementation. “Supports Atmos” must retain supported layout/processed-channel context.

## Power measurement

Do not compare wattage blindly. Preserve:

- watts per channel;
- load impedance;
- frequency range;
- distortion threshold;
- number of channels driven;
- test standard or manufacturer context.

Only score power directly when measurements are comparable or when a documented normalization method exists. Otherwise compare broader amplification capability and mark the raw wattage comparison unresolved.

## Requirement derivation

Material original functions create requirements when verified or user-confirmed, including:

- minimum amplified and processed channels;
- required speaker layout;
- necessary HDMI input/output count and present-day video modes;
- ARC/eARC use;
- required pre-outs or external-amplifier integration;
- room correction at an acceptable capability class;
- multi-zone behavior;
- phono/network features used by the system.

Present-day requirements may supplement the original—for example, modern HDMI/eARC needed for the current television—but must be labeled as user/system requirements rather than fabricated original specifications.

## Positioning and proximity

Use the legacy capability progression as context, not a rigid price ladder:

- entry home theater;
- mainstream surround;
- upper-midrange/enthusiast;
- premium AVR;
- processors plus separate amplification as a distinct architecture.

Newer HDMI technology does not automatically preserve the positioning of an older premium receiver. Compare amplification, processing, room correction, expansion, zones, connectivity, and model-series position together.

A current entry-level receiver may be an under-match despite newer video support. A current flagship may be `ABOVE LKQ` despite preserving every requirement. The closest candidate often combines comparable system capability with unavoidable modern connectivity.

## Fallback examples

1. Same architecture and system capability with routine modern HDMI changes.
2. Adjacent room-correction or connectivity implementation while preserving material system functions.
3. Loss of a verified but unused expansion feature only after user confirmation, with a cap.
4. Different channel count or architecture as a last-resort exception, normally `NOT LKQ`.

## Explanation checklist

Show amplified versus processed channels, supported layout, HDMI implementation, room correction, pre-outs, zones, power-measurement context, positioning, failed requirements, overshoot, fallback, and evidence. Do not reduce the explanation to channel count or wattage.
