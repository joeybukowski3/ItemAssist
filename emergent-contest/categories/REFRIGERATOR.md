# Refrigerator Category Rules

Refrigerator replacement is an installation and functional-configuration problem before it is a capacity contest.

## Relevant attributes

- configuration: top-freezer, bottom-freezer, side-by-side, French-door, column, or other
- width, height, and depth
- counter-depth versus full/standard-depth
- total capacity
- refrigerator and freezer capacity when available
- door count
- dispenser configuration and location
- ice-maker count and type
- finish
- smart capability where relevant
- generation-relative product positioning

Preserve measurement conventions. Product depth may be reported without doors, with doors, or with handles; installation guides may also require ventilation and door-swing clearance. Compare like measurements and retain their labels.

## Installation requirements

When cabinet opening, doorway, counter alignment, hinge clearance, or other fit constraints are known, model them as user-supplied or confirmed requirements. Width, height, and/or depth can be hard gates. A highly featured product that cannot be installed is `NOT LKQ`.

When the installation envelope is unknown, do not pretend dimensions are hard requirements. Display the uncertainty, request measurements, and use conservative provisional behavior defined by configuration.

Counter-depth/full-depth class often affects fit, capacity, and positioning and should not be collapsed into raw depth alone.

## Functional equivalency

- Preserve the major configuration unless a controlled fallback is required.
- Treat door count and convertible drawers as meaningful configuration, not automatic upgrades.
- Preserve a materially relevant dispenser and ice configuration.
- Treat finish as a softer requirement unless matching surrounding appliances or user constraints make it material.
- Smart features matter only when they were value-relevant to the original or required use.

The ItemAssist refrigerator example demonstrates the key principle: three products can share brand, approximate width, depth class, capacity, finish, smart capability, and dual ice, while a missing beverage feature under-matches and a premium four-door/FlexZone configuration over-matches.

## Capacity proximity

Capacity should meet minimum functional needs and remain close to the original. Larger is not always closer. Use a bounded/asymmetric distance function, and consider compartment capacity where the original use depends on freezer or refrigerator volume.

Do not allow advertised capacity to override installation failure or materially different configuration.

## Fallback examples

1. Close fit/configuration with a small, bounded capacity difference.
2. Cosmetic/finish substitution when the original finish is unavailable.
3. Controlled dispenser or ice configuration change only when exact functionality is unavailable and the difference is disclosed.
4. Different major configuration only as a last-resort best-available exception, usually not a true match.

## Explanation checklist

Lead with fit status. Show dimensions with measurement context, depth class, configuration, capacity distance, dispenser/ice differences, positioning, fallback, and sources. Separate verified product specifications from user-supplied installation constraints and separate both from the LKQ score.
